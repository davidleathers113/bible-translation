# 12. Efficacy Study Plan

> How to demonstrate — or honestly fail to demonstrate — the system's value to professional Bible translators. Methodology citations verified 2026-07-29 ([§3](03-research-and-prior-art-landscape.md)). Governing commitment, adopted from the brief: **productivity claims and quality claims are separate tracks; speed alone proves nothing about translation quality.**

## 12.1 Claims structure

| Track | Claim family | Primary measures |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Retrieval** | relevant prior work becomes discoverable | precision@5, recall (pooled), MRR, nDCG@5 vs. baselines |
| **B. Productivity/process** | recurring questions resolve faster; duplicated research drops; high-leverage work is identified | time-to-resolution, duplicated-research incidents, leverage-pick agreement |
| **C. Consistency** | inconsistent renderings decrease; justified variation is preserved | approved-rendering coverage per key term (Paratext-style), unjustified-variation counts |
| **D. Explainability/review** | documentation and consultant review improve | rationale coverage, consultant prep time, review-session efficiency |
| **E. Trust & burden** | translators trust, understand, and are not buried by it | Jian et al. trust scale, SUS, NASA-TLX (raw), capture-cost per record, adoption logs |
| **F. Quality (guarded)** | no quality regression; possible improvement | MQM-subset blind error annotation; consultant-check outcomes |

## 12.2 Study population and design realities

Qualified mother-tongue translators are few; teams, not individuals, are the working unit; no two projects are comparable; withholding a potentially helpful tool from partners has ethical costs. Therefore (per Shadish, Cook & Campbell 2002 on quasi-experimentation):

- **Within-subject / crossover designs** for controlled tasks (each translator is their own control; counterbalance *texts*, not people; carryover acknowledged as a limitation since precedent knowledge can't be washed out; mixed-effects analysis with random intercepts per translator and text; Green, Heer & Manning CHI 2013 is the design template).
- **Interrupted time series** on live projects (decisions/week, consistency coverage, tracked for months pre/post deployment).
- With N ≈ 5–20: report effect sizes and per-individual plots, prefer Wilcoxon signed-rank, treat p-values as secondary.
- **Ablation arms that separate structure from AI** (adapted from the companion ChatGPT context-engineering document): where session budget allows, controlled tasks run under (a) current practice, (b) the structured record/briefing views with **AI assists disabled**, and (c) the full system. Arm (b) is the critical one: if the benefit comes from structured capture and retrieval alone, the AI layer is optional garnish; if (c) ≫ (b), the assists carry real weight. Without this decomposition a positive result cannot say *what* worked.

## 12.3 Baselines

1. **Current practice**: Paratext notes + Biblical Terms + personal memory (the real competitor).
2. **TM-style baseline** for retrieval: fuzzy text match over decision text.
3. **Same-lemma-only baseline** for retrieval: Strong's-level lemma match — the naive version of the anchor idea.
The relevance-profile model must beat 2 and 3 to justify its complexity; the whole system must beat 1 on the workflow measures.

## 12.4 Protocols by track

**A — Retrieval (bench study, Phase 4).** Build a judged collection: sample 100–150 Issues from pilot corpora as queries; pool top-k from all systems/variants; 2–3 experienced consultants judge graded relevance (0–3) with a written rubric; adjudicate; report Krippendorff's α (target ≥ 0.67 before trusting results — below that, fix the rubric, per Artstein & Poesio 2008). Metrics: nDCG@5, MRR, P@5 per category; paired bootstrap for significance. Additionally instrument **live judgments**: treatment/dismissal events are graded relevance labels generated by normal use ([§5.8](05-relevance-and-precedent-framework.md)).

False positives are **categorized, not merely counted** (taxonomy adapted from the companion ChatGPT design document): same lemma / wrong sense; same construction / different discourse function; same language family / irrelevant typology; same theological label / different textual question; same rendering / different rationale; same project / obsolete policy; similar wording / different participant structure. Category frequencies feed directly back into per-dimension weight tuning ([§5.8](05-relevance-and-precedent-framework.md)) — a dimension that dominates one failure category gets down-weighted or demoted to display-only.

**Dependency-prediction evaluation.** For AI-suggested dependencies: confirmation rate (precision proxy) and consultant audit of a sample of *resolved* issues for dependencies the system missed (recall proxy); confusion analysis by dependency type. Error costs are **asymmetric** (per the companion ChatGPT design document): a false *blocking* suggestion wrongly halts work and erodes trust, so it is weighted more heavily than a missed soft dependency — scoring and any suggestion-threshold tuning must reflect that asymmetry.

**B — Productivity.** Event-log instrumentation from day one (issue opened → evidence viewed → decision saved; activity-gap thresholds to handle field interruptions, after the TPR pause-analysis tradition). Controlled component: matched recurring-question tasks (e.g., render a key term's 4th occurrence) with/without the precedent panel, crossover order. Live component: time-to-resolution distributions and duplicated-research incidents (two Issues on the same anchor researched independently — countable from the graph) over the time series.

**C — Consistency.** Operationalized in Paratext's own terms: approved-rendering coverage per key term before/after; **justified variation counts as consistent** (a rendering under a recorded `distinguished` treatment or sense split is not an inconsistency — this distinguishes the metric from mechanical 100%-sameness, which is not the goal). Morphology handled with wildcard/stem matching as Paratext does.

**D — Explainability/review.** Rationale coverage (% of approved Decisions with rationale + evidence links — near-zero baseline in current practice is itself a finding); consultant preparation time per pericope (self-timed, diary method); structured consultant interviews after checking sessions with and without decision-trail access.

**E — Trust and burden.** Jian, Bisantz & Drury (2000) 12-item trust scale administered longitudinally (report item-level, not just means — single-factor structure is contested); SUS after weeks 2 and 8 (benchmark 68); raw NASA-TLX around checking sessions; capture cost = median wall-clock per Tier-1 record from logs; automation-bias probes = acceptance rate of AI suggestions overall and for a small set of deliberately-seeded imperfect suggestions in controlled sessions only (with debrief; never in live project data). Instruments translated/adapted into the team's language of wider communication — an untranslated English SUS given to L2 users is its own validity threat.

**F — Quality (guarded).** Blind MQM-subset annotation (accuracy, terminology, and a project-defined acceptability category; severity-weighted) by consultants not involved in drafting, on stratified samples (key-term-dense pericopes oversampled); annotator agreement reported; consultant-check outcomes (issues raised per pericope) as a convergent measure. Blinding limit acknowledged: unusually consistent renderings can reveal condition; report this as a limitation rather than pretending otherwise.

## 12.5 Qualitative program

Semi-structured interviews (translators, consultants, team leads) at weeks 2/8/16; think-aloud usability sessions on the seven MVP screens; a diary channel inside the tool ("this suggestion was wrong because…"); analysis via thematic coding, with negative-case analysis mandated (search actively for the translator the system is failing).

## 12.6 AI-versus-human comparison protocols

Scoped narrowly to *assistive functions* (never renderings): (a) precedent-explanation quality — AI-drafted vs. human-written applicability explanations, blind-rated by consultants; (b) record-structuring — AI-drafted decision records from informal notes vs. translator-structured ones, rated for fidelity by the note's author (misrepresentation rate is the key safety number); (c) missing-dependency detection vs. consultant audit (from Track A). Renderings themselves are out of scope by design — the system does not translate.

## 12.7 Pre-registered success and failure criteria (Phase 4 wave; confirmatory wave 2 re-tests)

| Criterion | Success | Failure signal |
|---|---|---|
| Retrieval | nDCG@5 ≥ 0.6 and beats both baselines with meaningful margin | loses to fuzzy-text baseline → anchor thesis wrong or anchors too sparse |
| Capture cost | median Tier-1 record ≤ 90s; ≥60% of real questions captured | teams route around the system → capture problem unsolved |
| Recurring-question time | ≥25% median reduction in controlled tasks | no effect → precedent reuse doesn't pay at project scale |
| Consistency | coverage improves with no rise in *unjustified* variation | coverage up via rubber-stamping (variation drops where it was justified) → automation bias |
| Trust | stable/rising trust with stable acceptance | rising acceptance + falling trust, or acceptance of seeded-flaw suggestions ≥ control → bias alarm |
| Burden | NASA-TLX not worse than baseline sessions | tool sessions reliably higher-load → net burden |
| Quality | no MQM regression | regression → stop; speed is being bought with quality |

A negative result on the retrieval or capture criteria falsifies the buildable form of the thesis and must be reported as such — the study is designed so that failure is informative, not hidden.

## 12.8 Ethics and data handling

Informed consent covering logging granularity; community/organizational consent per partner norms (CARE-aligned, [§3.6](03-research-and-prior-art-landscape.md)); no draft text or decision content leaves the project without the sharing policy's say-so; participants can view and export their own logs; study protocols reviewed by partner orgs' research oversight (and an academic IRB if university collaborators join, which Track A's annotation program makes attractive).
