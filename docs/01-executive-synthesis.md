# 1. Executive Synthesis

## 1.1 The concept, restated

Bible translation is a long sequence of judgments — about senses, terms, constructions, discourse signals, cultural mismatches, and principles — made by teams over years, reviewed by consultants and communities, and today recorded (if at all) as prose notes scattered across Paratext threads, spreadsheets, and memory. The proposal: make each judgment a **structured, anchored, reviewable object**; make the relationships among judgments — dependency, precedent, constraint, supersession — **explicit and computable**; and use that structure to give translators three things they currently lack: *retrieval of relevant prior work with an explanation of why it's relevant*, *knowledge of what blocks what and what unblocking would be worth*, and *a decision trail that makes review and revision tractable*. AI assists throughout — retrieving, drafting, flagging — under an architecture in which it can never decide.

## 1.2 The strongest insight

The thesis's core claim — *translation decisions are not isolated; they share characteristics, constrain each other, and set precedents* — is *validated by the prior-art review* in an encouraging and clarifying way: every mature adjacent practice has independently evolved a partial version of it. Paratext's Biblical Terms registry is decision-reuse for one decision type (key-term renderings). Translation memory is precedent reuse at the wrong granularity (strings, not judgments). unfoldingWord's checkData invalidates checks when text changes — dependency tracking in embryo. Consultant checking is review workflow without a structured object to review. The insight that unifies them — **treat the decision, not the text segment or the term, as the first-class reusable unit, and connect decisions through shared anchors rather than manual links** — is genuinely novel in this domain as far as this review could determine ([§3.0](03-research-and-prior-art-landscape.md)), and it is the specific bet everything else in this suite serves.

The anchor formulation matters because it converts the thesis from a documentation aspiration into a computable claim: if decisions are anchored to shared identifier spaces (MACULA tokens, SDBH/SDGNT senses, key terms, typological features), then relevance is anchor intersection, dependencies propagate through anchors ("everything on this key term waits"), and the connections the brief wants "made explicit" are largely *derived* rather than hand-entered — which is what makes the economics of capture survivable.

## 1.3 The most important refinements to the original concept

1. **"Decision" decomposed.** A single decision object can't hold questions, options, evidence, and resolutions without either bloat or premature closure. The model uses an IBIS-shaped subgraph (Issue → Options/Arguments/Evidence → Decision → Reviews) presented to users as one simple card ([§2.3](02-terminology-and-conceptual-model.md), [§4](04-domain-model.md)).
2. **The R-scale rejected in favor of relevance profiles.** Relevance here is multi-dimensional and incommensurable; a scalar hides which dimension fired, invites automation bias, and mimics the E-scale's single axis where none exists. The replacement — typed facet profiles with a transient ranking score, per legal CBR's factor tradition — preserves the intent (estimating relevance distance) while staying explainable and correctable ([§5](05-relevance-and-precedent-framework.md)).
3. **The legal analogy kept for machinery, rejected for authority.** Applicability, distinguishing, treatments, and supersession transfer; binding precedent and jurisdictional hierarchy do not — external precedents are informative, never authoritative, and the community holds decision rights ([§2.4](02-terminology-and-conceptual-model.md)). This is not a courtesy: it is the design's answer to both theological-authority concerns and Indigenous-data governance (CARE) constraints.
4. **Dependencies made deterministic.** Dependency state is computed by rules over explicit data — never re-inferred by AI — with a small state machine, cycle tolerance via decision clusters, and staged, human-gated change propagation ([§6](06-dependency-and-prioritization.md)).
5. **The capture problem promoted to the central design constraint.** Forty years of design-rationale research says rationale capture fails on overhead. Hence: a five-field mandatory core, AI back-filling structure from informal notes, corpus seeding from existing Paratext data, and a pre-registered kill criterion on capture cost ([§7.4](07-user-experience-and-workflows.md), [§12.7](12-efficacy-study.md)).
6. **Sharing gated by governance, not by features.** Cross-project precedent — the concept's most exciting horizon — is also its most ethically loaded (community consent, weak anonymization in small language communities, revocation semantics). It is deliberately deferred behind a co-developed governance framework and in-project validation ([§5.7](05-relevance-and-precedent-framework.md), [§11 Phase 6](11-phased-roadmap.md)).
7. **Efficacy split into guarded claims.** Productivity, retrieval quality, consistency, explainability, trust, and translation quality are measured separately, with explicit failure criteria and the stated commitment that speed alone proves nothing about quality ([§12](12-efficacy-study.md)).

## 1.4 What would falsify the thesis

Stated up front because a research-grounded proposal owes its failure conditions: (a) the Phase-0 paper corpus shows real decisions *don't* exhibit dependency chains and reuse events at meaningful rates; (b) capture cost can't be driven under ~90 seconds for the common case; (c) anchor-based retrieval can't beat fuzzy-text matching on consultant-judged relevance. Any of these ends the project in its current form — and the roadmap is sequenced so each is tested about as early and cheaply as possible ([§11](11-phased-roadmap.md), [§14](14-next-actions.md)).
