## 3.0 Direct precedents vs. useful analogies

| | Direct precedents (systems doing part of this job today) | Useful analogies (models to borrow, not systems to integrate) |
|---|---|---|
| Decision capture | Paratext project notes & Biblical Terms renderings; translationCore checkData; unfoldingWord Translation Notes | Architecture Decision Records; IBIS/gIBIS; QOC; requirements traceability |
| Precedent retrieval | Translation-memory fuzzy matching; Paratext parallel passages | Case-based reasoning (Aamodt–Plaza); legal CBR (HYPO/CATO); citators (Shepard's/KeyCite) |
| Consistency checking | Paratext Biblical Terms checks; CAT-tool term QA; Greek Room; AQuA | — |
| Provenance | (largely absent in existing BT tools) | W3C PROV; event sourcing |
| Cross-language similarity | (absent) | Grambank/WALS typological vectors; Glottolog genealogy |

The overall **gap finding** of this review: *no located system — in Bible translation or the wider translation industry — stores adjudicated, scoped, rationale-bearing translation decisions as first-class interlinked objects with dependencies, supersession, and precedent retrieval.* (An absence claim is inherently unverifiable-negative; stated as "none found in this review." The closest commercial adjacency is Avodah's marketing of "governed workflows [where] every decision can be explained" — §3.6 — which signals demand, not an equivalent system.)

**Three design consequences.** (1) Paratext's Biblical Terms registry is the incumbent primitive decision store — *interoperate, never compete*: import/export renderings and notes. (2) translationCore's invalidation-on-edit and tN's quote+occurrence anchoring are field-proven answers to the "text moved under my annotation" problem — adopt both over character offsets. (3) Platform.Bible extension + Scripture Burrito extension are the two credible integration surfaces; both are open and active.

**Design consequences.** (1) The **open sense backbone** is SDBH/SDGNT senses + MACULA token IDs — anchor schemes can be built entirely on CC-licensed data; Louw–Nida *numbers* remain useful as a crosswalk where users hold licenses. ## 3.3 Rationale capture and decision records

| Resource | What it provides | Reuse | Key sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IBIS** (Kunz & Rittel 1970) | Issue / Position / Argument decomposition for "wicked problems"; gIBIS and Compendium operationalized it | concept — the Issue-centric decomposition adopted in [§2.3](02-terminology-and-conceptual-model.md) | [Working Paper 131 PDF](http://magrawal.myweb.usf.edu/phd/articles/ibis_wp_70.pdf); Compendium LGPL but unmaintained |
| **Toulmin model** (1958) | claim / grounds / warrant / backing / qualifier / rebuttal | concept — optional argument internals | [overview](https://www.utsa.edu/twc/documents/Toulmin_Model_of_Argumentation.pdf) |
| **QOC** (MacLean et al. 1991) | Questions–Options–Criteria design-space analysis | concept — Criteria ≈ scoring options against translation-brief principles | [HCI 6(3)](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1207/s15327051hci0603%25264_2) |
| **AIF** (Chesñevar et al. 2006) | Argument interchange ontology (I-nodes, RA/CA/PA schemes) | concept; map only if argumentation-tool interchange ever needed | [spec](http://www.arg-tech.org/wp-content/uploads/2011/09/aif-spec.pdf) |
| **Architecture Decision Records** (Nygard 2011; [adr.github.io](https://adr.github.io/)) | Minimal record (Title/Status/Context/Decision/Consequences); lifecycle `proposed→accepted→superseded`; MADR adds considered-options | concept — the **social proof** that short, append-only, supersession-based decision logs get used in practice | [templates](https://adr.github.io/adr-templates/) |
| **Requirements traceability** (DOORS tradition) | Typed directional links (satisfies/verifies/derivedFrom/refines) + **suspect-link flagging** on upstream change | concept — suspect links = the invalidation cascade of [§6.5](06-dependency-and-prioritization.md) | [IBM docs](https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/engineering-lifecycle-management-suite/doors/9.7.1?topic=requirements-links-traceability) |

**The capture problem.** Design-rationale research is candid that capture overhead killed gIBIS/QOC-style systems in practice ([survey](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-0-387-35175-9_29.pdf)): rationale capture succeeds only when it is a *by-product of work already being done*, and mandatory structure kills adoption. ADRs succeeded where design-rationale tools failed largely by being tiny and colocated with the work. This is the single most important prior-art lesson for this system, and it drives the "small mandatory core" principle ([§4.1](04-domain-model.md)) and the UX design ([§7](07-user-experience-and-workflows.md)).

## 3.4 Case-based reasoning and legal informatics

- **CBR** — Aamodt & Plaza (1994), "Case-Based Reasoning: Foundational Issues…", *AI Communications* 7(1):39–59 ([PDF](https://www.iiia.csic.es/~enric/papers/AICom.pdf)): the **Retrieve–Reuse–Revise–Retain** cycle *is* this system's operating loop; case = problem features + solution + outcome; retention turns every solved problem into a future case. Warning from the field: uncurated retention degrades the case base ("case-base maintenance" is its own subfield) — hence review states gate what becomes retrievable precedent.
- **HYPO / CATO** — Ashley's HYPO (dimensions; 3-ply argument: cite → distinguish → counter) and Aleven's CATO (boolean **factors** in a **factor hierarchy**; argument moves incl. *distinguishing* and *emphasizing/downplaying* distinctions). Survey: Bench-Capon, "HYPO's Legacy," *AI and Law* 2017 ([PDF](https://www.csc.liv.ac.uk/~tbc/publications/hypoLegacy.pdf)). Direct inspiration for the **relevance profile** ([§5.2](05-relevance-and-precedent-framework.md)): factor overlap for retrieval, factor difference for principled distinguishing.
- **Citators** — Shepard's / KeyCite treatment signals (followed, distinguished, criticized, limited, overruled) with visual health flags ([Hellyer, *Law Library Journal* 110(4) 2018](https://www.aallnet.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/LLJ_110n4_02_hellyer.pdf)): the model for precedent **treatments** and health display ([§5.6](05-relevance-and-precedent-framework.md)). Reuse the idea, not the trademarked names.
- **Limit of the analogy:** legal precedent operates within one jurisdiction's authority structure; translation precedent crosses languages where similarity must discount typological distance, and no decision binds. [§2.4](02-terminology-and-conceptual-model.md) audits this in full.

## 3.6 AI in Bible translation (2023–2026) and human-in-the-loop research

Verified landscape (names and attributions corrected during research):

- **AQuA** ("Augmented Quality Assessment", SIL; [aqua.sil.org](https://aqua.sil.org/); [sil-ai/aqua-api](https://github.com/sil-ai/aqua-api), active, last push 2026-07) — AI QA copilot: flags accuracy/clarity/naturalness issues, verse-level heat maps; framed as consultant "power tools."
- **Greek Room** (USC ISI + Wycliffe USA — *not* Biblica/UBS; [greekroom.org](https://greekroom.org/)) — encoding checks, alignment-based spell-checking without curated wordlists, word-alignment-based terminology-consistency visualization; user study: [arXiv 2302.00778](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.00778).
- **silnlp** ([sillsdev/silnlp](https://github.com/sillsdev/silnlp) — under sillsdev, not sil-ai) — MT pipelines for resource-poor languages; the drafting side.
- **BibleNLP** ([ebible corpus](https://github.com/BibleNLP/ebible)) — open parallel Bible corpus aligned with MACULA.
- **ETEN Innovation Lab** ([etenlab.org](https://www.etenlab.org/)) — innovation funnel; AI-across-the-process agenda.
- **Avodah** ([avodah.com/connect](https://www.avodah.com/connect)) — commercial; markets "governed workflows… every decision can be explained, defended, and trusted"; vendor time-reduction claims (75–80%) should be treated as marketing.

**Positioning:** AQuA and Greek Room *detect* problems; silnlp/ATT and Scripture Forge *draft* text. None captures **why** a rendering was chosen or reuses adjudicated decisions. The proposed system is complementary infrastructure: detection flags become Issues; drafting output is constrained by Decisions; decision records give the AI layer something trustworthy to retrieve.

**Domain literature on AI and human authority:** the SIL-hosted **Bible Translation Conference** publishes proceedings that include sustained practitioner debate on AI drafting and translator formation — verified: [proceedings index](https://btconference.org/proceedings) and, e.g., "Lost in Translation: Navigating the Intersection of AI and the Sanctity of Drafting in BT" ([2023 proceedings](https://btconference.org/2023-proceedings/lost-in-translation-navigating-the-intersection-of-ai-and-the-sanctity-of-drafting-in-bt)), which argues that bypassing the drafting process for speed removes friction the community formation process needs. Three further 2025 titles are cited in the companion ChatGPT document — "The Privilege of First Draft," "A Study of Equivalence in Methods of Using AI-Generated Drafts," and "More Than Machines: Human-Centered Ethics in AI in Bible Translation" — [unverified: the specific pages could not be confirmed through this session's proxy; check btconference.org directly]. This literature is the domain-internal grounding for the suite's human-authority stance (§8): the debate is live among the system's intended users, which is itself a reason the AI layer must remain optional and inspectable.

**Human-in-the-loop findings that shape the design:** interactive/adaptive MT (CASMACAT; Lilt's cross-segment adaptation — the closest analogue to precedent propagation, but at model-weight level, uninspectable); QE4PE ([arXiv 2503.03044](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.03044)) as a controlled-study template for "does surfacing flags change behavior/quality"; and the priming / "shining-through" literature (professionals miss errors in fluent NMT output — [Informatics 6(3):41](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9709/6/3/41)) which motivates treating decision capture as a *deliberate-consideration forcing function*. A canonical "automation bias in translation" literature is thin; the general trust/bias instruments are specified in the efficacy plan (§12.4, Track E). Transfer caveat: nearly all HITL research is on high-resource pairs with professional translators; transfer to mother-tongue-translator settings is an open question and a stated limitation of the efficacy design.

**Data governance:** **CARE principles** (Carroll et al. 2020, [Data Science Journal](https://datascience.codata.org/articles/10.5334/dsj-2020-043); [gida-global.org](https://www.gida-global.org/careprinciples)) — Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics — explicitly correcting FAIR's sharing-optimism. Decision records encode community theological/cultural judgments — arguably Indigenous data; **cross-project precedent sharing is exactly the secondary reuse CARE regulates**. Design implications adopted in [§5.7](05-relevance-and-precedent-framework.md): per-community opt-in, revocability, consent at publication, confidentiality tiers (Paratext's confidential-project practice is the field precedent). No formal published SIL/Wycliffe/UBS community-data-ownership policy was found [unverified-negative].

**Translation copyright:** most translations are restrictively copyrighted; open exceptions usable as reference/pivot texts: World English Bible (PD), Berean Standard Bible (PD/CC0 as of 2023), unfoldingWord ULT/UST (CC BY-SA). Storing quotations from licensed model translations inside decision records creates licensing exposure — a records-policy item, [§13](13-risks-and-open-questions.md).

**Translation process research:** Krings' think-aloud tradition and the PACTE competence model legitimize the "translation decision" as a unit of analysis and predict decision support helps less-experienced translators most; CRITT TPR-DB ([overview](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/300634156_The_CRITT_Translation_Process_Research_Database)) supplies methods (not representative data — no located TPR work on mother-tongue Bible translators [unverified-negative]).

| **W3C Web Annotation Data Model** ([spec](https://www.w3.org/TR/annotation-model/), Recommendation 2017) | Standard model for attaching annotation bodies to targets via **selectors** (TextQuoteSelector, TextPositionSelector, fragment selectors…) | **map** — the anchor model ([§4.3.3](04-domain-model.md)) serializes to WA selectors for interchange: quote+occurrence anchors ≈ TextQuoteSelector; token-ID anchors ride alongside as a custom selector | Selectors over *mutable* drafts still need our revision-ID discipline — WA gives the interchange form, not the edit-resilience strategy (which comes from the tN quote+occurrence pattern, §3.1) |
| **OWL 2 / SHACL** | Ontology reasoning / RDF graph validation | concept; SHACL only if an RDF projection exists (graph-level invariants like "superseded ⇒ exactly one successor") | SHACL 1.2 WG re-chartered through 2026 |
| **JSON Schema (2020-12)** ([spec](https://json-schema.org/draft/2020-12)) | Record-level validation | direct — validate every record at write time, offline | Enormous validator ecosystem |
| **Property graphs / GQL** | Neo4j (Community GPLv3, single-instance; JVM-heavy), Memgraph (BSL, in-memory), **Apache AGE** (openCypher on Postgres, Apache 2.0, active), ISO **GQL** published 2024 | AGE = server-side graph option; write queries to openCypher for portability | **Kùzu is dead**: repo archived read-only 2025-10-10 (verified on [github.com/kuzudb/kuzu](https://github.com/kuzudb/kuzu)); press reported the archiving followed Apple's acquisition of Kùzu Inc. | **Event sourcing / CQRS** ([Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html)) | Append-only event log + projections; audit and temporal queries for free | concept/architectural — scope to the decision log only, not the whole app | Events map 1:1 to PROV Activities |

## 3.9 Discourse-analysis representation (brief's capability 9)

The question: which discourse frameworks offer **controlled vocabularies** usable as structured annotation, versus interpretive methods that would create false precision if encoded as data?

### Assessment of frameworks

| Framework | Controlled vocabulary? | Verdict for this system |
|---|---|---|
| **Levinsohn / Longacre (SIL tradition)** ([Discourse Features of NT Greek](https://www.sil.org/resources/publications/entry/386); [narrative self-instruction materials, 2023](https://www.sil.org/resources/publications/entry/95483) — applied to 500+ languages) | Partial — recurring analytical categories, no machine-readable inventory | **Primary seed vocabulary.** These categories (participant-reference patterns, connective functions, fore/background, highlighting devices, constituent-order variation, reported-speech framing) are what SIL-trained consultants already use; codifying them as a versioned SKOS scheme would be a novel contribution requiring SIL engagement |
| **Runge / Lambrecht information structure** (Runge, *Discourse Grammar of the GNT*; Lexham Discourse GNT — whole-corpus device-level annotation, proprietary) | Yes, small (topic/focus/frames) | Adopt category set conceptually; LDGNT data is license-blocked; pin one definition set with a crosswalk (topic/theme/given collide across schools) |
| **PDTB-3 sense hierarchy** ([LDC2019T05](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/LDC2019T05); manuals open) | Yes — 4 top-level / 14 second-level senses; connective-anchored, shallow, local | **Adopt the hierarchy** (not the corpus) for the connective-rendering decision class (καί/δέ/γάρ/οὖν…) — cheaper and more reliable than full trees |
| **ISO 24617-8 DR-core** ([iso.org](https://www.iso.org/standard/60780.html); open papers) | Yes — framework-neutral core discourse relations bridging PDTB/RST/SDRT | Neutral interlingua tier behind the field-facing vocabulary; ISO text paywalled, inventory documented in open papers |
| **RST** (Mann & Thompson 1987 — [PDF](https://www.sfu.ca/rst/pdfs/Mann_Thompson_1987.pdf)) | Yes (~23 relations) but inventory varies by author; full-tree annotation expensive; modest inter-annotator agreement | Relation labels usable *without* mandatory trees; a stored RST tree presents one interpretation as fact — false-precision risk |
| **Halliday & Hasan cohesion** | Yes (5 types: reference, substitution, ellipsis, conjunction, lexical) | Adopt for cohesive-tie annotations (participant-reference chains) |
| **Searle speech acts** ([1976](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-in-society/article/abs/classification-of-illocutionary-acts1/C51AB2FEB1BFD115FE9B066A8669789D)); ISO 24617-2 dialogue acts | Yes (5 classes / 9 dimensions) | Searle's five classes: high-value, low-cost, teachable (rhetorical question, blessing-as-declaration…). Full DiAML: built for interactive dialogue — cherry-pick only |
| **Relevance Theory / Gutt** ([SIL page](https://sil.org/contributor/gutt-ernst-august); Wendland's assessment — [PDF](https://translation.bible/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/wendland-1996-on-the-relevance-of-relevance-theory-for-bible-translation.pdf)) | No — explanatory theory; but it is the dominant post-Nida SIL framework | **Source of the rationale taxonomy**, not text annotation: a small enumerated set of decision-rationale types (implicature made explicit / contextual-assumption gap / processing-effort reduction / explicature preserved…). No existing standard — original contribution, marked as such |
| **DRT / Parallel Meaning Bank** ([PMB](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.03964)) | Formalism, not taxonomy | Concept only: PMB's meaning-graph-aligned-across-languages pattern is architecturally instructive; full DRS annotation is far too heavy for field teams |
| **SFL (full)** | No (full interpretive grammar) | Cohesion subset only; wholesale adoption invites unbounded annotation scope |

### Recommended representation per concept class

| Concept | Representation |
|---|---|
| Participant-reference pattern, connective sense, speech-act class, cohesion type, prominence device | **Controlled vocabulary terms** (SKOS; Levinsohn/Runge-seeded, crosswalked to PDTB/ISO DR-core) — used as discourse anchors |
| "This connector realizes relation X between spans A and B" | **Graph relationship** (annotation edge between text anchors), carrying annotator + confidence |
| Why a rendering was chosen (RT-style reasoning) | **Rationale-type tag** (small controlled set) **+ free-text explanation** — never a bare number |
| Genre/text-type of a pericope | **Structured observation** from a canonical per-pericope map (project-overridable) |
| Focus structure, thematic prominence, rhetorical intent | **Confidence-scored annotation, attributed** — interpretive judgments recorded as claims by someone, never as intrinsic text properties |
| Language-specific discourse phenomena not in the seed vocabulary | **Project-specific extensions** (SKOS extension scheme, flaggable for promotion into the shared vocabulary) |

**False-precision guardrail** (applies across the board): every discourse annotation is an *attributed claim with confidence and annotator identity*. Inter-annotator agreement on discourse categories is historically modest even among experts; the schema must preserve disagreement rather than launder judgment into fact.

## 3.10 What this landscape implies

1. **Build on, don't rebuild:** text substrate (USFM/USJ + versification mappings), anchor ID-spaces (MACULA, SDBH/SDGNT, Glottolog, Grambank), delivery surfaces (Platform.Bible extension, Scripture Burrito), governance vocabulary (FOBAI, CARE), evaluation instruments (MQM, IR metrics, trust scales) all exist with usable licenses.
2. **The genuinely novel parts** — and therefore the research contributions to validate: the decision subgraph model, anchor-mediated relevance profiles, deterministic dependency state, the treatment vocabulary applied to translation, and the machine-readable codification of the Levinsohn-tradition discourse vocabulary.
3. **The two hard non-technical constraints** surfaced by research: the design-rationale capture problem (adoption) and CARE-governed sharing (cross-project scope). Both shape the MVP boundary ([§10](10-mvp-definition.md)).
